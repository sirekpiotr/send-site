Why it isn't a writing assistant

It commits to an answer.

Ask a chatbot whether to send something and it will tell you that it depends, that both options have merit, and that you should do what feels right. That is not help. That is the same paralysis with more words.

SEND? picks one. Send it or don't, and a sentence naming the actual thing — the apology you opened with, the three days you mentioned, the question you never got round to asking. You can disagree with it. You cannot come away with nothing.

What you get

A verdict, then the evidence.

The answer, in one word

DON'T SEND in red, or SEND IT in green, with one line saying why — specific to this message, not to messages in general. Under it, the single reading that explains the verdict: the loudest thing in what you wrote, as a number.

Your own words, marked up

The draft comes back with the lines that cost you highlighted where they sit — so “sorry to bother” is not an item in a list further down, it is the third phrase of the thing you are about to send, glowing. Each one quoted back with the reason it lands badly.

Six ways to say it

Confident, direct, casual, mysterious, warm and funny — and you only get the ones that suit this message, not six for the sake of six. Every concrete detail you wrote is kept: the place, the day, the plan. Nothing invented on your behalf.

Your texting type

Sixteen types.
You are one of them.

Check four messages and SEND? works out what kind of texter you actually are — from your scores, your hours, who you write to and how you type. Then it hands you a badge and you can argue about it in the group chat.

It reads none of your messages to do it. Every number behind a type is counted on your phone as you go and nothing you wrote is kept. There is no history here to go back through, which is the whole point.

✨ Your type unlocks on your fourth check

One type, not a collection

You do not unlock these. You are one of them, and it only changes when you do — a type that flipped every time you checked a message would be a mood ring, not a personality. Tap the badge, turn it over in your hand, send it to whoever needs to see it.

Pricing

Three checks, free.

Every free check gets the verdict and the reason. Pro unlocks the rest, on every message: the exact phrases dragging it down, whether they are likely to reply, the thread counts, every rewrite — and your texting type.

Pro is $3.99 a week or $24.99 a year, which works out at about 48 cents a week. Prices vary by country. It renews until you cancel, and you cancel in the App Store rather than by emailing us.

Privacy

Nobody reads this but you.

There is no account and no sign-up, so there is nothing for a message to be attached to. Nothing you type is stored — a draft exists for as long as it takes to judge it, and then it is gone. There is no history to go back through, by design.

A full analysis is read once by our model provider and held briefly by them, tied to nothing that identifies you. Screenshots of a conversation are read on your phone; the picture is never uploaded. The full detail is in the privacy policy.
